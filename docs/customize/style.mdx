---
title: "Teach DocWriter your writing style"
---

Give DocWriter a few passages of your writing, then choose which patterns you want the agent to follow. The analysis takes about **ten minutes** but can take longer.

The screenshots use public domain passages by [Alice Meynell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Meynell) from [*The Rhythm of Life and Other Essays*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1276). Use your own writing when you set this up. References describe how you write. Use [writing rules](/customize/rules) to tell the agent what to do, and set an [intended audience](/agent/customize#set-the-intended-audience) to name the reader.

## 1. Add 3 to 5 sources

Open the references pill in the header, or go to **Settings**, then **Writing references**. Add 3 to 5 passages from your writing. For each source:

- Paste a few hundred words. A sentence or two is not enough.
- Add a short description, such as "paper introduction" or "related work section".
- Select **Add source**.

<Frame caption="Step 1. Three sources are ready to analyze.">
  ![The Calibrate your style dialog on the Sources step with three pasted passages listed](/images/style-guidance-sources.png)
</Frame>

Then select **Analyze**.

## 2. Run the analysis

Select **Run analysis**. DocWriter measures your passages, then three specialist agents study them. A final pass combines their findings into instructions. The analysis uses the model selected in the DocWriter header.

You can keep writing while the analysis runs. Use the left column to follow its progress, and select any step to see its notes or measurements.

<Frame caption="Step 2. You can follow the progress while the analysis runs.">
  ![The Analyze step showing Lexis, Grammar, and Discourse specialists in progress](/images/style-guidance-specialists.png)
</Frame>

## 3. Choose the version that sounds like you

Each card compares a passage from your sources with a version that changes one writing choice. Choose:

- **A** or **B** when one version sounds more like you.
- **Both are the same to me** when you have no preference.
- **Neither is good** when you want to edit one version.
- **Skip proposition** when you do not want the agent to follow that choice.

<Frame caption="Step 3. Compare the two versions and choose the one that sounds more like you.">
  ![Preference cards with passage A and passage B for style propositions](/images/style-guidance-preference.png)
</Frame>

Answer the cards until none remain. DocWriter may add an instruction without a comparison when your passages provide strong evidence for it.

## 4. Review and save your style instructions

Open **Style draft** and remove any instruction you do not want. Select **Finalize style** when the draft looks right. The writing agent will not use a new or updated draft until you finalize it.

<Frame caption="Step 4. Review the instructions before you finalize them.">
  ![The Active skill step listing style propositions with example passages](/images/style-guidance-skill.png)
</Frame>

You can also download the skill or restore an earlier version. To discard the current analysis, return to **Analyze** and select **Start over**. Your sources and last finalized skill will remain.

## 5. Use your style instructions

After you finalize the skill, ask the writing agent to write or edit as usual. You can put the request in a directive inside the document:

```text
[[ Write two short paragraphs about waiting beside a late train in the rain, sounding like me. Keep the scene ordinary. Replace this directive. ]]
```

Wake the agent, or wait for the idle timer. The edit appears as a tracked change with an **Accept**, **Reject**, or **Retry** card. Open the card to review the full edit.

<Frame caption="Step 5. Review the agent's edit and decide whether it sounds like you.">
  ![The editor with an expanded review card for a style-matched rewrite about a late train](/images/style-guidance-editor-edit.png)
</Frame>


## How it works

We designed DocWriter’s style calibration feature around four properties:

1. **Specific to your writing.** The guidance should describe patterns in your writing, rather than generic advice about good writing.
2. **Important to you.** The guidance should include only the patterns you want the agent to preserve.
3. **Easy for you to confirm.** You should be able to make decisions without reviewing a long list of abstract claims.
4. **Portable across agents.** The guidance should work with writing agents outside DocWriter.

We discuss each property in turn.

**Specific to your writing.** We base the analysis on the linguistic checklist from Leech and Short’s [*Style in Fiction*](https://www.routledge.com/Style-in-Fiction-A-Linguistic-Introduction-to-English-Fictional-Prose/Short-Leech/p/book/9780582784093). The specialist agents examine words and short phrases, sentences and punctuation, and how ideas connect across a passage. They analyze headings, lists, and other layout choices separately. An agent proposes a pattern only when it can quote an example from your sources. We also discard measurements that provide no evidence. For example, a passage with no citations says nothing about how you use citations.

**Important to you.** We build on Shaikh et al.’s [*Creating General User Models from Computer Use*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10831), where knowledge and preferences about a user are stored as structured propositions with confidence scores. We adapt the proposition format to writing and tie every proposition to quoted examples. A pattern found in your sources may not be one you want the agent to follow, so you confirm each proposition before it becomes part of your writing guidance.

**Easy for you to confirm.** The most direct way to confirm a proposition would be for DocWriter to show you the proposition and ask whether you agree. However, a long list of abstract claims is hard to evaluate, especially when you cannot see how each claim changes the writing. Instead, DocWriter shows you a passage from your sources next to a version where one proposed pattern has changed. You choose the version that sounds more like you. If neither version sounds right, you can edit one or skip the proposition.

**Portable across agents.** After you finish, the propositions you approved are packaged as an `author-style` skill under `.docwriter/skills/author-style/`. You can download the skill bundle and use it with other agents that load and use skills, to then follow the same guidance and examples.
